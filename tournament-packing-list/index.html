        Skip these between games

        Fast food burgers and fries, chips as the main snack, candy, donuts, and energy drinks. They sit heavy or crash their energy right when the next game starts.

        Keep cheese, yogurt, milk and sandwiches on ice. Toss anything perishable that has been out of the cooler for more than 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F). Check the team's allergy list before you share snacks.

        How the planner works

        The gap between games decides what your athlete can eat. A stomach needs time to empty before hard play, so the shorter the gap, the lighter and simpler the food.

        • Under 45 minutes: water and quick carbs only. Orange slices, half a banana, a few pretzels, an applesauce pouch.
        • About an hour: a small snack right after the game ends. A granola bar, half a bagel, crackers and a cheese stick.
        • Two hours or more: a real mini meal right away. A turkey sandwich, fruit and milk or yogurt, then sips of water until warmups.
        • After the last game: carbs plus protein within an hour. Chocolate milk and a sandwich is the classic for a reason.

        Amounts are per athlete with a couple of extras built in, because siblings and teammates always show up hungry. For exact fluid amounts by weight, use the free hydration calculator.

        General guidance for healthy kids, not medical or nutrition advice. If your athlete has a food allergy, diabetes or another medical condition, follow their pediatrician's plan.
