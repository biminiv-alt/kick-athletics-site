The rule for snack week

Bring one item from each box below for every player, plus 3 extras. Water is the drink.

1 · Fruit
  • 1 clementine, or orange slices (about half an orange)
  • Half to 1 banana
  • 1 cup of grapes or watermelon (halve grapes for players under 5)
  • 1 applesauce pouch
2 · Carb
  • 1 small bag of pretzels (about 1 oz)
  • 1 plain mini bagel
  • 2 graham cracker sheets or 2 fig bars
  • 1 nut-free granola bar
3 · Protein
  • 1 cheese stick
  • 1 yogurt tube (frozen ones thaw by the final whistle)
  • 1 single-serve milk or chocolate milk (8 oz), kept cold
  • 1 turkey or beef stick

Portions are per player. Under 8: the smaller end. Middle school and up: a full banana or a second carb.

When to hand it out

  • Halftime: water first. Orange slices if your league does halftime fruit. Keep it light so stomachs are ready for the second half.
  • After the game: hand out the snack in the post-game huddle. The first 30 to 60 minutes after playing is the best time to refuel.
  • Tournament days: between games, a small carb, a piece of fruit and water. Save the bigger snack for after the last game.

Drinks

  • Every player brings their own full water bottle.
  • Sports drinks are for long, hot days and tournaments, not a 1-hour game.
  • Skip juice pouches, soda and energy drinks.

Skip these as the snack

  • Donuts, cookies and cupcakes
  • Chips as the main item, and candy
  • Anything with nuts, unless every family has said OK
Easiest snack week

Clementines, cheese sticks and small pretzel bags. Nut-free, kid-approved and easy on the budget. Swap the cheese if the team has a dairy allergy.

Allergy-friendly swaps

Nuts / peanutsFruit, pretzels and nut-free granola bars. Read the "may contain" line: many bars are made near nuts.
DairyTurkey sticks or roasted chickpea snack packs instead of cheese, yogurt or milk.
GlutenRice cakes and gluten-free pretzels or crackers instead of bagels, pretzels or graham crackers.
Sesame / eggPlain mini bagels only (skip sesame and "everything"). Check labels on any bakery item.

Before the first game: ask the coach or team parent for the allergy list. Store-bought, individually wrapped items with labels are safest. Never send homemade food if a player has a severe allergy.

Cooler and cleanup

  • Keep cheese, yogurt and milk on ice in a small cooler.
  • Toss perishables that have been out of the cooler for more than 2 hours (1 hour if it's above 90°F).
  • Bring a trash bag. The other parents will notice.
