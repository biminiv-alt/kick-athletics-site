Holiday tournaments: what to eat over the break

Holiday tournaments land in the middle of the busiest eating week of the year. Heavy dinners, cookies on every counter, late nights with cousins and then an 8 AM game. A little planning keeps the holiday fun and the legs fresh.

The big family meal the night before

A holiday dinner can be a great pre-game meal. Turkey, ham, potatoes, rolls and pasta are exactly the carbs and protein a young athlete needs. The trouble is usually the portion size and the timing.

  • A normal plate, not three plates, the night before a game
  • Potatoes, rolls, stuffing and lean meat are all good choices
  • Go easy on very rich, creamy or fried sides late at night
  • Save most of the dessert table for after the last game

Late nights and early games

Sleep matters as much as food. If the first game is early, set a bedtime that gives them a full night, even with family in town. Here is why a short week of sleep shows up on the field.

Breakfast on game day

Eat 2 to 3 hours before warmups. Leftovers work well here.

  • Toast or a bagel with peanut butter and a banana
  • Eggs with leftover potatoes
  • Oatmeal with fruit
  • A turkey sandwich on a roll if the game is late morning

Between games

Holiday events often run long, with big gaps between games. Pack small, familiar snacks for short gaps and a real meal for gaps of 2 hours or more. Here is how to fuel two games in one day.

  • Orange slices, clementines, grapes or bananas
  • Pretzels or crackers
  • Half a leftover turkey sandwich
  • Water all day, plus a sports drink for long or hot sessions

Travel days

Many holiday tournaments mean a long drive or a flight. Pack the cooler at home so the team is not living on gas station food. Here is a full guide to tournament travel and hotel food.

Holiday cooler

Two water bottles each, a sports drink, clementines, bananas, pretzels, cheese sticks, leftover turkey sandwiches and chocolate milk for the ride home.
