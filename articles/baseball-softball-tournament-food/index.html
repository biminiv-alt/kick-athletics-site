Baseball and softball tournaments: what to pack and eat

Baseball and softball tournaments are long. Games run over, start times slide, and players spend hours in a hot dugout. The kids who stay sharp in the last game are usually the ones who kept eating and drinking all day.

Before the first game

A real breakfast about 2 to 3 hours before the first pitch.

  • A bagel or toast with peanut butter and a banana
  • Oatmeal with fruit
  • Eggs, toast and juice

Early start? A smaller breakfast in the car works. Here is what to eat before an early game.

What goes in the dugout cooler

  • Two water bottles per player, kept cold
  • A sports drink for hot afternoons
  • Orange slices, grapes or watermelon
  • Pretzels, crackers and granola bars
  • Half sandwiches for longer gaps
  • Sunflower seeds are fine, but they are not food

Between innings

Every half inning in the dugout is a chance to drink. A few sips each time adds up. Kids who wait until they feel thirsty in July heat are already behind.

Between games

Short gap: snacks and fluids. Longer gap: a light meal like a sandwich, fruit and crackers. Skip the big greasy plate from the concession stand right before the next game. More on eating between games.

Hot weather is the big one

Summer tournaments on turf or dirt fields get very hot. Shade between games, cold water, salty snacks, and a sports drink on hot days all help. Here is more on hot weather games.

Tournament morning

Real breakfast 2 to 3 hours before first pitch, two cold bottles in the cooler, fruit and pretzels for the dugout, sandwiches for the gap.
