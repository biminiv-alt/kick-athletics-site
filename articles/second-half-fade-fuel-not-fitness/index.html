Why your athlete fades in the second half. It is fuel, not fitness.

By Kick Athletics · September 28, 2026 · 5 min read

Your athlete looks sharp in the first half. Then somewhere between the whistle and the halftime cup of water, something changes. The step gets slower. The pass goes long. The tackle is a second late. By the last twenty minutes they are a different player.

Parents assume the answer is conditioning. More running. More sprints. Longer practices. That is the wrong frame. Second-half fade in youth athletes 9 to 15 is almost always fuel, not fitness.

What is actually happening

A youth athlete carries roughly 60 to 90 minutes of ready-to-burn glycogen. That is the fast fuel their muscles run on for high-intensity efforts. Sprints, tackles, jumps, reactive plays. Once that tank is depleted, the body switches to slower fuel sources and the athlete does not have the tools to override it.

The outward signs look like fitness. Heavy legs. Slower reactions. Bad decisions in the last third. A parent watching from the sideline sees a kid who "gassed out" and thinks conditioning. What actually happened is that the tank was underfilled at kickoff and there was no in-game fuel to keep it topped off.

Why more running does not fix it

You cannot condition your way out of an empty tank. A well-fueled athlete with average conditioning outperforms a fasted athlete with elite conditioning every time. Youth sports research has been consistent on this for two decades and it is why elite academies structure their pre-training meal timing with the same precision as their drill schedule.

Youth athletes are especially vulnerable because their glycogen stores are smaller relative to body weight, and because most families do not treat pre-game eating as part of performance. Cereal is not a game-day breakfast. A granola bar in the car on the way to the field is not a pre-game meal.

The three-window fix

Three hours before the whistle

A real plate. Palm-sized protein (eggs, turkey, chicken). Fist of carbs (oatmeal, toast, rice, a bagel). One piece of fruit. Water. This is the foundational fill and it needs 2 to 3 hours to move out of the stomach so it is not sloshing around during warmups.

Sixty to ninety minutes before

A small carb top-off. A banana. A slice of toast with honey. A rice cake with a thin spread of peanut butter. Not a snack bar with 15 grams of protein. Not a shake. Something the body can convert to available fuel fast without asking the gut to work hard.

Halftime

Half a banana. A few orange slices. A small handful of pretzels. Water. This is the difference between fading and finishing. Most kids drink at halftime and eat nothing, which is why the tank runs dry between minutes 50 and 75.

What to skip

Anything sold at the concession stand. Sports drinks with more sugar than fuel. Nutrition bars marketed to adults. A big meal 45 minutes before kickoff. Skipping breakfast because "he is not hungry." A hungry youth athlete on game day is a fading youth athlete by minute 55.

Why this is a fixable gap

Every family we talk to who runs the three-window fix for two weeks reports the same thing. The kid stops fading. Coaches stop pulling them at minute 60. They finish games with something in the tank.

This is not the answer for every athlete. If your kid is fading and they are already fueling well, the gap is somewhere else. It might be sleep debt. It might be hydration. It might be that they are actually undertrained. That is what the Performance Check is for.
