Healthy breakfast for teen athletes

Many teen athletes skip breakfast, then wonder why they drag through afternoon practice. Breakfast is the first fuel of a long day: school, then a 2 hour practice, then homework. It does not need to be fancy. It needs carbs for energy, some protein to stay full, and something to drink.

What a good athlete breakfast includes

  • Carbs: oats, toast, bagels, cereal, waffles, fruit
  • Protein: eggs, yogurt, milk, cheese, peanut butter, turkey
  • A drink: milk, water or juice

15 fast breakfast ideas

  • Oatmeal made with milk, topped with banana
  • Scrambled eggs and toast
  • Bagel with peanut butter and a glass of milk
  • Greek yogurt with granola and berries
  • Cereal with milk and a piece of fruit
  • Whole grain waffles with yogurt
  • Egg and cheese breakfast sandwich
  • Breakfast burrito with eggs, cheese and beans
  • Overnight oats made the night before
  • A smoothie with milk, yogurt and frozen fruit
  • Toast with peanut butter and banana slices
  • Leftover rice or pasta with an egg
  • Cottage cheese with fruit and toast
  • Hard-boiled eggs, a muffin and fruit
  • A turkey and cheese English muffin

No time? Grab and go

Keep a few things ready for rushed mornings: a banana and a cheese stick, a yogurt and a granola bar, or a PB&J in a bag. Eating something on the bus beats eating nothing until lunch.

Before morning practice or a game

When practice starts early, eat a smaller, lighter breakfast 30 to 60 minutes before, like toast with honey or a banana, then a bigger breakfast after. More on early morning games. For game days, here is the game day breakfast plan.

Simple rule

Every breakfast has a carb, a protein and a drink. Ten minutes is enough.
