What to eat before practice

Most youth athletes eat lunch around 11 or noon and walk onto the practice field around 4 or 5. That is five or six hours on whatever was in the lunchbox, and plenty of kids barely touched it. By warmup they are running on empty, and it shows in the last half hour of practice.

The gap nobody plans for

Families plan dinner. Families plan game day. The after-school window between the last bell and practice is the one that gets left to chance: whatever is in the car, the vending machine, or nothing at all. Practice is where most of the season's work happens, so it deserves a plan too.

Timing

  • 60 to 90 minutes before practice: a real snack. Carbs plus a little protein
  • 30 minutes or less before: keep it small and simple. Mostly carbs, easy to digest
  • Straight from school to the field: eat in the car, and choose from the "quick" list below

Snacks that work 60 to 90 minutes out

  • A turkey or peanut butter sandwich
  • Yogurt with granola or fruit
  • A bagel or English muffin with a little cheese or nut butter
  • Crackers and cheese with a piece of fruit
  • A bowl of cereal with milk
  • Leftover rice or pasta from last night's dinner

Quick options for the car

  • A banana or a handful of grapes
  • Applesauce pouch
  • Pretzels or a plain granola bar
  • Fig bars or graham crackers
  • Dried fruit

These are mostly carbs, which is the point. Carbs are the fuel your athlete burns at practice intensity, and they leave the stomach faster than fat or a big serving of protein.

What to skip right before practice

  • Greasy fast food. It sits heavy and can mean side stitches and an upset stomach
  • Big portions of anything. A snack, not a second lunch
  • Energy drinks and soda. Caffeine and a sugar spike are not a fuel plan
  • Very high-fiber or gassy foods if your athlete has a sensitive stomach
  • Anything brand new. Test new foods on a rest day

Do not forget the water bottle

The after-school window is also where hydration falls apart. Many kids drink almost nothing at school. A full bottle in the backpack, and a few big swallows before warmup, puts them on the field ahead instead of behind. More on how much in how much water a youth athlete should drink.

Late practice?

If practice runs from 6 to 8, your athlete may need two small snacks: one right after school and one quick carb an hour before. Then dinner after practice becomes the recovery meal. Here is what that recovery meal should look like.

This week

Pick two "before practice" snacks your athlete actually likes, one real snack and one car option. Keep a supply in the house and the car so practice days are not a scramble.
