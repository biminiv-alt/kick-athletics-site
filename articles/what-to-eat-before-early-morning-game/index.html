What to eat before an early morning game

The 8 a.m. game with a 7:15 warmup is a fueling problem with no clean answer. Nobody wants a full breakfast at 5:30, and a kid who wakes up at 6:15 does not have three hours to digest. So most families skip it. Then their athlete runs out of gas halfway through.

Start the night before

When the morning is short, dinner does more of the work. A normal dinner with a bit more carbs, plus a small bedtime snack like cereal with milk or toast with peanut butter, means your athlete wakes up with more in the tank. Here is what that dinner looks like.

The early-game rule: smaller and simpler

The closer the meal is to warmup, the smaller and plainer it should be. Mostly carbs, a little protein if there is time, very little fat or fiber.

If there is 90 minutes or more before warmup

  • A bagel or toast with a little peanut butter and a banana
  • Oatmeal with honey or fruit
  • Yogurt with granola
  • A bowl of cereal with milk and fruit

If there is less than an hour

  • A banana
  • Applesauce pouch
  • Half a bagel or a plain granola bar
  • A few crackers or pretzels
  • A small glass of juice if solid food will not go down

Eat in the car

For the earliest games, breakfast happens in the car. Pack it the night before so nobody is digging through the pantry at 6 a.m.

Drink something right away

Kids wake up a little behind on fluids. A cup of water or milk as soon as they are up, and a few sips on the drive, helps more than most parents expect. More on how much water a youth athlete needs on game day.

What to skip

  • Greasy breakfast sandwiches on the way in
  • A big plate of eggs and bacon right before warmup
  • Energy drinks or coffee drinks
  • Skipping it entirely

Plan the top-off

An early game means a small breakfast, so halftime matters more. A few orange slices or half a banana at the break helps them finish. Here is what works at halftime.

This week

If your athlete has an early game coming up, pack a car breakfast and a water bottle the night before, and add a small bedtime snack.
