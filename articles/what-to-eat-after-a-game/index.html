What to eat after a game

The final whistle is when most families stop thinking about food. It is also when your athlete's body starts repairing muscle and refilling the energy they just burned. What they eat in the next hour or two shapes how they feel at the next practice.

What recovery actually needs

Three things, in this order:

  • Fluids to replace what they sweated out
  • Carbs to refill the energy stored in their muscles
  • Protein to help repair the muscle they worked

Protein gets the attention, but carbs do most of the refilling. A plate of chicken with no rice or bread leaves the tank half empty.

Timing

Aim for a snack or meal within about an hour of finishing. If dinner is more than an hour away, bridge it with a recovery snack in the car, then have a normal dinner at home.

Easy recovery snacks

  • Chocolate milk (a simple carb-plus-protein option most kids will drink)
  • Yogurt with fruit or granola
  • A turkey, ham or peanut butter sandwich
  • Cheese stick, crackers and a piece of fruit
  • A bowl of cereal with milk
  • Trail mix with dried fruit (if there are no nut allergies)

The recovery dinner

Same idea as the night-before plate, with protein pulling a bit more weight:

  • Carbs: rice, pasta, potatoes, tortillas or bread
  • Protein: chicken, fish, lean beef, eggs, beans or tofu
  • Color: vegetables or fruit they will actually eat
  • A drink: water or milk

Tacos, a rice bowl, spaghetti with meat sauce, or breakfast for dinner all work. For daily protein numbers by weight, read how much protein a youth athlete needs.

Fluids after the game

Most kids finish a game behind on fluids, and many keep falling behind on the drive home. Get a bottle in their hand before they reach the car. After a hot game or a lot of sweat, salty food at dinner helps replace what they lost. If cramping is a pattern, read why cramping is a sodium problem.

Tournament weekends

Between games, "recovery" and "fuel for the next game" are the same meal. Keep it light, carb-focused and familiar, and save the big protein meal for the end of the day. Here are the five between-game mistakes most families make.

What to skip

  • Skipping it entirely because they are "not hungry." A small snack still counts
  • Drive-through as the default. Fine once in a while. Not the recovery plan
  • Soda or energy drinks in place of water or milk
This week

Put a recovery snack and a full bottle in the bag before every game and practice this week. Hand them over at the final whistle, before anyone gets in the car.
