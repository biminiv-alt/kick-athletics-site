The tournament kit that keeps your athlete alive in game 3

By Kick Athletics · September 16, 2026 · 4 min read

Youth tournaments are designed around two economics. The tournament makes money from teams paying to enter. The concession stand makes money from parents who did not pack food.

Nothing about that setup is designed to fuel a 12-year-old across three games in one day. If you have ever watched your athlete cook in game 1, look OK in game 2, and completely fall apart in game 3, this is a big part of why.

What is in most tournament food

Pretzels. Nachos. Gatorade. Candy. Maybe a hot dog. Occasionally something that resembles a real sandwich. Almost never real protein. Almost never real fruit. Never enough water.

A 12-year-old eating pretzels and Gatorade between games is running on sugar and salt. It works for about 45 minutes. Then it does not. The blood sugar crash lands right about when game 3 kicks off.

The tournament kit that works

Pack this the night before. Put it in a soft cooler with an ice pack. Keep it in the car or at the field. Do not rely on the concession stand.

The base

  • 2 turkey or peanut butter sandwiches on whole wheat. One for between games 1 and 2. One for between games 2 and 3.
  • 2 bananas. Fast, easy, real potassium, real carbs.
  • 2 cheese sticks. Protein that survives a warm cooler.
  • 1 bag of pretzels or trail mix. The salt actually helps here because they are sweating. Just do not make this the whole meal.

Fluids

  • 32 oz water bottle, frozen the night before if it is going to be hot. Refill at every water fountain on the field.
  • One small electrolyte packet (Liquid I.V., LMNT, or a Pedialyte packet). Mix into a water bottle after game 2, not before. This is when they actually need electrolytes.

The extras that make a difference

  • A small bag of grapes or blueberries. Something wet and cold that they will actually eat when they are hot and tired.
  • A hand towel. Not a food thing, but wiping their face and neck with a cool towel between games actually helps them recover faster.
  • A folding chair. If they have a chair to sit in with their feet up between games, their heart rate drops faster and they recover better.

The timing template

Assuming three games with 90-minute breaks between them:

  • Right after game 1: Water immediately. Sandwich within 30 minutes. Cheese stick and banana within the next 30 minutes.
  • Between games 2 and 3: Electrolyte water. Second sandwich. Handful of grapes or pretzels. Chair time.
  • After game 3: Real recovery meal within 60 minutes of the final whistle. This is where your day-of nutrition either compounds or crashes.

What to skip

  • Big meals right before games. Their stomach cannot process a full meal in 30 minutes. Cramping is not "just conditioning."
  • Sugar-forward energy drinks. They spike blood sugar fast and crash it faster. This is the fastest way to make game 3 unwatchable.
  • Anything they have never tried before. Tournament day is not the day to test a new protein bar.

What actually happens when you do this

The first tournament you pack the kit, your athlete will notice something is different. Maybe they will not say so. But you will see it in game 3. The kids who ran out of fuel two hours ago will be slower. Yours will not.

By the second or third tournament this becomes routine. They start asking you for the cheese stick. They start hydrating without being reminded. The habit locks in.

This one change closes more of the youth sports performance gap than any supplement or camp.

Next tournament

Pack the base kit above tonight. Do not rely on the concession stand. Watch how your athlete looks in game 3 vs how their teammates look. The difference is not subtle.
