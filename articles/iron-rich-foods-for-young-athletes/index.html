Iron-rich foods for young athletes

Iron helps carry oxygen to working muscles. Young athletes need plenty because they are growing and training at the same time. Teen girls, endurance athletes like runners and swimmers, and vegetarian athletes need to pay extra attention.

Iron-rich foods kids will actually eat

  • Meat and fish: lean beef, turkey, chicken thighs, tuna
  • Eggs
  • Beans and lentils: chili, bean burritos, lentil pasta sauce
  • Fortified cereals and oatmeal
  • Tofu and edamame
  • Spinach, best cooked into pasta, eggs or smoothies
  • Pumpkin seeds and peanut butter

Help the body absorb it

Pair iron foods with vitamin C: orange slices with cereal, strawberries with oatmeal, peppers in chili, tomato sauce with lentils. Try not to drink milk or tea with an iron-rich meal; have milk at a snack instead.

Easy iron meals

  • Fortified cereal with strawberries
  • Beef or turkey tacos with salsa
  • Chili with beans and peppers
  • Pasta with meat or lentil sauce
  • Egg and spinach scramble with orange juice

Vegetarian athletes

Plant iron is harder to absorb, so build beans, lentils, tofu and fortified cereal into most days, always with a fruit or vegetable high in vitamin C. More on feeding a vegetarian athlete.

Tired all the time?

Ongoing tiredness, getting winded easily or a sudden drop in performance can have many causes. Talk with your pediatrician before giving iron supplements; they can check whether your athlete needs one.

Simple rule

One iron food at most meals, paired with fruit or vegetables high in vitamin C.
