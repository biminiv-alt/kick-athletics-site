Cold weather games: fuel and fluids

Cold games bring their own problems. Kids do not feel thirsty, cold hands make it hard to open snacks, and long waits on a freezing sideline burn energy just keeping warm.

They still need water

Young athletes sweat under layers, and cold, dry air pulls water out with every breath. Because the cold blunts thirst, many kids drink far less than they need. Set a simple rule: a few sips at every break and every substitution. The free hydration calculator gives a target for their weight.

Warm drinks count

A thermos of warm water, warm milk or a warm sports drink is easier to drink on a cold sideline than an icy bottle. Skip energy drinks and keep hot chocolate as a treat for after the game.

Breakfast and pre-game meal

Same rules as any game day: carbs plus a little protein 2 to 3 hours before warmups. Warm food goes down well in the cold.

  • Oatmeal with a banana
  • Eggs and toast
  • Pancakes or waffles with fruit

Snacks between games

Pick snacks that are easy to eat with gloves or cold fingers.

  • Bananas
  • Granola bars, opened ahead of time
  • Pretzels in a zip bag
  • A thermos of soup or pasta for long gaps
  • Half a sandwich wrapped in foil

After the game

Get them warm and dry first, then feed them: chocolate milk, a sandwich or a warm meal within an hour. Here is what to eat after a game.

Cold sideline bag

A thermos of warm water or warm sports drink, a regular water bottle, bananas, pre-opened granola bars, pretzels, a thermos of soup or pasta, and dry layers for after.
