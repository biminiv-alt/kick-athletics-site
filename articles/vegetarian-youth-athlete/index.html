Feeding a vegetarian youth athlete

Plenty of young athletes play well on a vegetarian diet. The two things to plan for are getting enough protein through the day and eating enough total food, because plant meals can be filling without carrying as many calories.

Easy protein sources

  • Eggs, Greek yogurt, milk and cheese (if your athlete eats dairy and eggs)
  • Beans, lentils and chickpeas
  • Tofu, tempeh and edamame
  • Peanut butter and other nut or seed butters
  • Whole grain bread, pasta and oats, which add more than people expect

Spread protein across meals and snacks instead of saving it for dinner. Here is how much protein a youth athlete needs.

The pre-game meal: 3 to 4 hours before

  • Pasta with marinara and cheese
  • A rice and bean bowl with a little cheese
  • A bagel with eggs and fruit
  • A peanut butter and banana sandwich with yogurt

Keep beans and very high fiber foods modest right before a game, since they can sit heavy for some kids.

Game day snacks

  • Bananas, grapes and applesauce pouches
  • Pretzels, crackers and granola bars
  • String cheese or a yogurt tube
  • A PB&J cut in quarters

After practice or a game

Chocolate milk, Greek yogurt with granola, a bean and cheese quesadilla, or a smoothie with milk and peanut butter all give carbs and protein together. More on what to eat after practice.

What to watch for

If your athlete seems tired all the time, is losing weight, or a fully plant-based diet cuts out dairy and eggs too, it is worth a check-in with their pediatrician about iron, vitamin B12 and overall intake.

Simple vegetarian game day

Pasta with marinara and cheese 3 to 4 hours before, fruit and pretzels before warmups, water all day, and chocolate milk or yogurt after.
