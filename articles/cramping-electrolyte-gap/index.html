HYDRATION

Cramping is not just a water problem. It is a sodium gap.

By Kick Athletics · September 28, 2026 · 5 min read

Your athlete cramps in the second half. You hand them water. Sometimes it helps. Often it does not. And the pattern keeps repeating even when they are drinking plenty. So you assume it must be conditioning, or bad genetics, or just how their body works.

Cramping in youth athletes is not just a water problem. It is almost always a sodium and electrolyte problem, and water alone can make it worse, not better.

What is actually happening in a cramp

Muscle cramps in athletes are triggered when the electrolyte balance around the muscle fibers goes out of range. The three that matter most are sodium, potassium, and magnesium. Sodium is by far the biggest lever, especially in kids who sweat heavily.

A hard youth game can drain 500 to 1,500 milligrams of sodium out of the body in sweat. If your athlete drinks only water to replace what they lost, they dilute the sodium concentration in their blood even further. The cramp gets more likely, not less.

Why "just drink Gatorade" is not the fix

Most sports drinks marketed to youth athletes have far more sugar than sodium. A standard 20-ounce Gatorade has about 270 milligrams of sodium and 34 grams of sugar. For a kid who just sweated out 1,000 milligrams, that is a third of what they lost and a big blood sugar spike they did not need.

The result is a kid who feels temporarily better because of the sugar, then feels worse in the next quarter because the sodium gap is still there and now the blood sugar is dropping.

The salty kid signal

If your athlete leaves crusty white lines on their forehead, jersey collar, or the brim of their hat after games, they are a heavy sodium sweater. This is not about hygiene. It is a real physiological signal that they lose more sodium per liter of sweat than the average kid.

Salty sweaters need more aggressive sodium replacement than the average recommendation. They also cramp earlier, cramp harder, and cramp more often. Recognizing this pattern is half the fix.

The fix that actually works

Before the game

A pinch of table salt in their water bottle 30 minutes before warmup. Or a low-sugar electrolyte mix (LMNT, Liquid IV, or similar) with a real sodium load of 500 to 1,000 milligrams. Not a sports drink from the store shelf.

During the game

Small sips of water at every stoppage. If it is hot or the game is long (over 60 minutes of hard play), half a bottle of an electrolyte mix at halftime. Not a full bottle of a sugary sports drink.

After the game

Water plus real food with salt. Chicken and rice. Pretzels. A turkey sandwich. Chocolate milk. The sodium in normal meals refills the tank without needing supplements.

The label to read

For a hydration drink to actually prevent cramps in a heavy-sweating youth athlete, it needs at least 500 mg of sodium per serving and low sugar. Most brands on the sports drink aisle fail one or both. Look at the nutrition panel, not the marketing.

Why this is worth fixing

Cramping takes your athlete off the field at the exact moment the game matters most. It also builds a mental pattern where the kid starts to expect cramps in the third quarter and pulls back on effort to avoid triggering one. That is a performance ceiling almost nobody diagnoses.

The sodium gap is one of the most fixable inputs in youth sports. It is not talent. It is not conditioning. It is a nutrition label. Ten minutes of setup on Saturday morning changes what happens at minute 55.
