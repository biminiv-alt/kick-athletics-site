Winter break sports camp: what to pack

By Kick Athletics · November 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Winter break camps and clinics pack a lot of work into a few days: drills in the morning, games in the afternoon, and short breaks in between. Kids who show up fed and bring the right bag stay sharp through the last session.

Breakfast before camp

Most camps start early. Aim for a real breakfast about 1 to 2 hours before drop-off.

  • Oatmeal with a banana and a spoon of peanut butter
  • Eggs and toast
  • A bagel with cream cheese and fruit
  • Greek yogurt with granola

A lunch that works for a long afternoon

Lunch breaks are short and the afternoon is usually the hardest part. Pack carbs, some protein and something salty.

  • A turkey or chicken wrap, or a peanut butter and jelly sandwich
  • Pasta salad or a thermos of pasta
  • Pretzels or crackers
  • Fruit: apple, grapes or clementines
  • Milk or chocolate milk if there is a fridge or ice pack

Snacks for short breaks

Camps often give 5 to 10 minute breaks. Easy, quick snacks keep energy up without a heavy stomach.

  • Granola bars or fig bars
  • Applesauce pouches
  • String cheese
  • Trail mix (check the camp's nut rules first)

Water, even in the cold

Kids sweat in winter too, especially in a warm gym or under layers outside, and they feel less thirsty in the cold. Send a full water bottle for the morning and one for the afternoon. Here is how much water a youth athlete needs.

After camp

A snack within an hour helps them recover for the next day: chocolate milk, a sandwich, or yogurt and fruit. Then a normal dinner and an early night, since most camps run several days in a row.

Camp bag

Two water bottles, a packed lunch with an ice pack, two snacks for breaks, a sports drink for long afternoons, and a recovery snack for the ride home.
