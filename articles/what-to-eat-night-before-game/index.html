What to eat the night before a game

The night-before dinner is the meal families overthink and underplan at the same time. Some go all-in on a giant pasta feed. Others grab drive-through on the way home from practice. Neither sets your athlete up well for the morning.

Why dinner the night before matters

The energy your athlete runs on in the first half is mostly stored the day before. Carbs eaten at dinner (and through the day) top up the energy stored in their muscles, which is what they burn hard in a game. Breakfast tops it off. It cannot fill an empty tank by itself.

The plate that works

Think "normal dinner, slightly more carbs, nothing new."

  • Half the plate: carbs they like. Pasta, rice, potatoes, bread, tortillas. A bit more than usual, not double
  • A quarter: lean protein. Chicken, turkey, fish, lean beef, eggs or beans
  • A quarter: cooked vegetables or fruit. Something familiar
  • A glass of water or milk

Chicken and rice, spaghetti with meat sauce, a burrito bowl, or a baked potato bar all fit. You do not need a special recipe.

What to skip the night before

  • Anything new. Game eve is not the night to try a new restaurant or a new food
  • Very greasy or fried food. It sits heavy and can mean a rough night and an unsettled stomach in the morning
  • Huge portions. "Carb loading" for a youth game does not mean eating until they hurt
  • Lots of gassy, high-fiber extras if your athlete has a sensitive stomach
  • Caffeine from soda, tea or energy drinks. It costs sleep, and sleep is the other half of game-day prep

Timing

Aim to finish dinner two to three hours before bed so they are not trying to sleep on a full stomach. If dinner is early or practice runs late, a small snack before bed helps: a bowl of cereal with milk, toast with peanut butter, or a banana. More on why sleep matters so much in the two-week sleep debt.

Tournament weekends

Before a multi-game day, the night-before carbs matter even more, and so does packing. Get the cooler ready the night before so breakfast and between-game food are not a scramble. Here is the tournament kit that keeps your athlete going in game 3.

Do not forget fluids

Steady drinking the evening before means your athlete wakes up ahead instead of behind. A glass with dinner and a bottle nearby in the evening is plenty. For the full game-day fluid picture, read how much water a youth athlete should drink on game day.

This week

Pick one go-to night-before dinner your athlete likes and that fits the plate above. Use it before every game for the next few weeks so it becomes routine, not a decision.
