TOURNAMENTS

Tournament weekends. The five between-game mistakes and the cooler list.

By Kick Athletics · September 28, 2026 · 6 min read

Two-day tournament weekend. Four games. Maybe five if you bracket in. Your athlete looks sharp Saturday morning, decent Saturday afternoon, off Sunday morning, and looks like a different kid by the semifinal Sunday afternoon.

That is not conditioning. That is not talent. That is the between-game window. Tournament fatigue in youth athletes is almost always about the four to six hours between games, not the games themselves.

The five between-game mistakes

1. Concession stand as the meal plan

Pretzels, pizza slices, sports drinks, candy. Everything sold trackside is engineered to be profitable, not to fuel a 12-year-old going into their next game in three hours. A pizza slice at 11am is a heavy stomach at 1pm and a heavy body by minute 40.

2. Nothing in the first thirty minutes after a game

The recovery window opens the second the whistle blows. Real carbs and some protein within 30 minutes rebuilds the glycogen tank for the next game. Most kids drink half a Gatorade and lie in the shade. That is the biggest recovery mistake in youth sports.

3. Underhydrating between games

A youth athlete loses 1 to 2 liters of water in a hard game. Most kids replace less than half between games. By game 3 they are running on a body that is 3 percent below hydrated, which is where reaction time collapses and cramping starts.

4. The sugar spike

Skittles or a soda between games feels like fuel because it tastes like fuel. What actually happens is a fast blood sugar spike, a sharp drop 45 minutes later, and a kid who feels flat during the warmup for the next game.

5. Sitting in the car with the AC on

Between games in hot weather, some families camp in the parking lot with the AC blasting. The body cools too fast, the legs stiffen, and the warmup for the next game is fighting cold muscles. Shade, a chair, water, and a walk every 20 minutes is the actual recovery move.

The cooler list

Pack the night before. Not the morning of. Morning-of packing is why families end up buying pretzels at the concession stand.

Cold side

  • Two turkey and cheese sandwiches on whole wheat, cut in halves
  • Grilled chicken strips in a small container
  • Greek yogurt cups (two, plain or vanilla)
  • Cheese sticks (four)
  • Baby carrots and cucumber slices
  • Grapes and orange slices in a sealed container
  • Chocolate milk (two 8-ounce bottles, for the 30-minute post-game window)
  • Water bottles (three, frozen the night before so they thaw slowly)

Room temperature

  • Two bananas
  • A bag of pretzels
  • Two rice cakes
  • A small jar of peanut butter and a spoon
  • A sleeve of graham crackers
  • Two small applesauce pouches
  • A few plain snack bars (something like RXBAR or a simple oat bar, not a candy bar)

Hydration

  • A large jug of water for refills
  • One bottle of an electrolyte mix (Liquid IV, LMNT, or a similar sodium-forward option) per day
  • Chocolate milk within 30 minutes of every game finish
The timing that matters

Within 30 minutes of finishing a game: chocolate milk plus real carbs. Ninety minutes before the next game: a small carb top-off. Twenty minutes before warmup: a few sips of electrolyte water and a piece of fruit. Everything else is comfort.

Why this is worth the packing

Tournament weekends are where families spend the most money and see the least return. Hotel. Gas. Registration. Coach fees. And then the athlete plays four games as four different kids because the between-game window was left to the concession stand.

Fixing the cooler list is the single highest-return change you can make for your athlete's tournament performance. It also happens to be the cheapest one.
