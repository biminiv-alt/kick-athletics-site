Hockey tournaments: what to eat at the rink

Hockey tournaments mean early ice times, long drives and rink food that is mostly nachos and hot chocolate. Players work hard in short shifts in heavy gear, and they sweat more than parents realize even in a cold rink.

Early ice times

A 6 or 7 am game is common. Eat something small in the car: a bagel with peanut butter, a banana, a yogurt or a granola bar, plus water. Here is more on early games.

Later games: the real meal

About 3 hours before puck drop: pasta, a rice bowl, a turkey sandwich with fruit, or pancakes and eggs. Then a small snack about an hour before getting dressed.

What to pack for the rink

  • Two water bottles, one for the bench
  • A sports drink for long or back-to-back games
  • Bananas, oranges or grapes
  • Pretzels, crackers and granola bars
  • Sandwiches for longer gaps
  • Chocolate milk for after the game

The cold rink trap

Kids do not feel thirsty when the air is cold, but full gear makes them sweat hard. Water on the bench between shifts, and a full bottle after every game.

Between games

Short gap: a snack and fluids. Long gap: a real lunch that is not fried. Rink nachos and candy right before the next game usually show up as heavy legs. More on eating between games.

Travel weekends

Most hockey tournaments mean a hotel. A grocery run the first night beats eating every meal at the rink or a restaurant. Here is how to do it.

Hockey bag

Two bottles, fruit, pretzels, granola bars, a sandwich and chocolate milk. Water between shifts even when the rink is freezing.
