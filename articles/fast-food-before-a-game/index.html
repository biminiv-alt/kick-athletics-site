Fast food before a game: the best drive-thru picks

Every sports parent ends up at a drive-thru on game day. That is fine. A fast food meal can fuel a game well if you order with the game in mind. The goal is the same as any pre-game meal: easy carbs, a little protein, not too much fat or grease, and plenty of fluid.

The simple rule

Pick grilled over fried, a bun or wrap over a basket of fries, and water or milk over soda. Then eat it 2 to 3 hours before the game if you can. If the game is in an hour, eat half and save the rest for after.

Good picks by place

  • Sandwich shops: a turkey or chicken sub on regular bread, light on sauce, with a bag of baked chips or fruit
  • Chicken places: a grilled chicken sandwich or nuggets with a fruit cup instead of fries
  • Burger places: a plain hamburger or small cheeseburger, apple slices and milk
  • Mexican places: a bean and rice burrito or a chicken burrito bowl with rice, easy on cheese and sour cream
  • Breakfast drive-thru: an English muffin egg sandwich, oatmeal, or a bagel, plus a banana from home

What to skip right before a game

  • Large fries and fried baskets: slow to digest and often sit heavy
  • Big milkshakes and large sodas: lots of sugar, little that helps on the field
  • Spicy or very greasy sauces that can upset a nervous stomach
  • Energy drinks: not for kids, ever

Between games at a tournament

If there are 2 hours or more between games, a small fast food meal works. With less than 2 hours, go lighter: half a sandwich, fruit, pretzels and water. Here is how to fuel two games in one day.

After the game

This is the best time for the treat. A burger and fries after the last game is fine. Add chocolate milk or a glass of milk for recovery. Here is what to eat after a game.

Drive-thru order before a game

A grilled chicken sandwich or turkey sub, a fruit cup or apple slices, and a bottle of water. Save the fries and the shake for the ride home.
