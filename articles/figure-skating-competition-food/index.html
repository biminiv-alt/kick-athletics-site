What a figure skater should eat on competition day

Figure skating competitions mean early call times, long waits and a short, intense program. Nerves often kill appetite, so many skaters eat too little. But jumps and spins take real energy, and a skater who has not eaten can feel shaky and tired on the ice.

Breakfast: 2 to 3 hours before skating

  • Oatmeal with banana
  • A bagel or toast with peanut butter
  • Eggs and toast
  • Yogurt with granola and fruit

Keep it familiar. Competition day is not the time for a new food.

Snacks around warmups

About 30 to 60 minutes before warmup, a small, easy snack helps: half a banana, a few crackers, an applesauce pouch or a granola bar. If nerves are bad, try a little juice or a sports drink instead of food.

Long waits between events

Pack a snack bag for the rink: fruit, pretzels, crackers, a sandwich and water. If there are hours between skates, have a light meal in the middle of the wait.

Drinking in a cold rink

Cold air makes kids feel less thirsty, but they still sweat in costumes and warmups. Have them sip water through the day. More on staying fueled in the cold.

A note on food and skating

Skating can put a lot of focus on body shape. Kids skate and grow best when they eat enough every day. If you are ever worried about how your skater is eating, talk to their doctor.

After skating

A real meal or snack with carbs and protein: chocolate milk, a sandwich, pasta or whatever the family is having. More on recovery food.

Simple competition day

A familiar breakfast, a small snack before warmups, a packed snack bag for the waits, water all day, and a real meal after.
