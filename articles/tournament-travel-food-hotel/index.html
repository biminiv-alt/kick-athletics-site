Away tournaments: feeding your athlete from a hotel room

Away tournaments are where game-day fueling falls apart. No kitchen, early check-out, a hotel breakfast that is mostly pastries, and a team dinner at a restaurant with a long wait. A little planning fixes most of it.

Make one grocery run when you arrive

Skip the gas station and the hotel lobby shop. One trip to a grocery store on the first night stocks the room and the cooler for the whole weekend.

  • Bagels, bread and peanut butter
  • Bananas, apples, oranges and grapes
  • Yogurt cups and string cheese
  • Pretzels, crackers and granola bars
  • Deli turkey for sandwiches
  • Chocolate milk
  • A case of water

The hotel breakfast

Most hotel breakfasts have what your athlete needs if you skip the pastry tray.

  • Oatmeal with fruit or honey
  • A bagel or toast with peanut butter
  • Yogurt and granola
  • Eggs if there is time before warmup
  • Juice or milk

If the game is early, grab a bagel and a banana to eat in the car. More on early games here.

Between games away from home

This is what the cooler is for. Sandwiches made in the hotel room that morning, fruit, pretzels and drinks beat a long line at the concession stand. Here is how to handle the gap between games.

The team dinner

Team dinners are fun and they matter, but they are often late and heavy. Steer toward:

  • Pasta with a simple sauce
  • Rice bowls, burritos or fajitas
  • Grilled chicken with a potato or rice
  • Bread or rolls

Go easy on fried appetizers and huge desserts when there is a game in the morning. A small bedtime snack back at the hotel helps if dinner was early or small.

Do not forget sleep

Late nights in the hotel pool add up. Food sets up the next day, and so does getting to bed. More on sleep here.

Before you leave

Pack the cooler, a loaf of bread, peanut butter and a water bottle. Plan the first-night grocery run on your phone map.
