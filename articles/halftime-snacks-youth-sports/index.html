Halftime snacks that actually help

Halftime is short. Your athlete has a few minutes to catch their breath, hear the coach and get back out there. A snack at halftime is not a meal. Its only job is to top off energy for the second half without upsetting their stomach.

Does my athlete even need a halftime snack?

For a short game after a good breakfast or lunch, water alone is often enough. A quick carb at halftime earns its place when:

  • The game is long or intense
  • It is hot and your athlete is sweating heavily
  • Their last real meal was more than three or four hours ago
  • It is the second or third game of the day
  • They tend to fade late in games (read why the second-half fade is usually fuel, not fitness)

The best halftime snacks

Quick carbs, small portions, nothing that needs a lot of chewing:

  • Orange slices. The classic for a reason: carbs and fluid in one
  • Banana halves
  • Grapes or watermelon chunks
  • Applesauce pouches
  • A few pretzels
  • Fruit snacks or a couple of gummy chews
  • A few sips of sports drink on long, hot days

How much is enough?

A few orange slices or half a banana. Enough to top off, not enough to feel full. If your athlete eats a lot at halftime, they will feel it in the first ten minutes of the second half.

Fluids come first

The most useful thing at halftime is usually the water bottle. A few big swallows at every break adds up. On hot days and tournament days, fluids plus a little salt matter even more. Here is how much your athlete should be drinking on game day.

Leave these in the cooler

  • Chips and other greasy snacks
  • Protein bars and anything heavy or chewy
  • Candy bars with chocolate and nuts
  • Soda and energy drinks
  • Large sandwiches (save them for after the game)

This week

Pack one halftime option and a full water bottle for every game. Watch the last ten minutes of the game and see whether your athlete has more left than usual.
